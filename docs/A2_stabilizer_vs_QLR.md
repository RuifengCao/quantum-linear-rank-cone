# The five-party stabilizer entropy cone is strictly smaller than the QLR cone

Technical note accompanying round R27/R28 of this repository. Everything stated
as a computation is reproducible with the scripts and data named in brackets;
everything stated as a proof is proved here. Status flags: **[proved]** — proof
in this note; **[cited]** — standard result, reference checked; **[certified]** —
exact rational certificate, re-checked by an independent implementation
(`epr1kit/stabcert.py`, gate G20). Human line-by-line review of this note is
pending.

## 1. Setting

Parties A, B, C, D, E and a purifier F. For a pure state on ABCDEF the entropy
vector S is determined by the 31 values S(X), ∅ ≠ X ⊆ {A,…,E} (mask order in
the data files), via S(X) = S(X^c).

*QLR₅* is the cone of Bao, Cheng, Hernández-Cuenca and Su (arXiv:2002.05317,
§2.3): the five-variable linear rank inequalities of Dougherty–Freiling–Zeger
(arXiv:0910.0284), with classical monotonicity replaced by weak monotonicity and
all orbits completed under the S₆ symmetry of von Neumann entropy. In this
repository it is `data/qlr5_H_facets10860.npy` (31 classes, 10,860 facet
instances; matched class by class with the DFZ-derived list in round R12).

*Stab₅* is the closed convex cone generated by entropy vectors of stabilizer
states of prime local dimension p on ABCDEF (each party may hold any number of
qudits). Stab₅ ⊆ QLR₅ [cited: Gross–Walter, arXiv:1302.6990, Thm 2 —
stabilizer entropies obey every balanced inequality valid for the associated
phase-space random variables].

BCHS (arXiv:2006.16292, §4) remark that "there is significant evidence for and
none against the coincidence of the stabilizer and QLR cones".

## 2. Two lemmas

**Lemma 1 (normal form) [proved].** If S is the entropy vector of a stabilizer
state, then S is also the entropy vector of a stabilizer state in which every
party P holds exactly S(P) qudits.

*Proof.* Let G be the stabilizer group (order p^N) and, for a set Y of qudits,
G_Y the subgroup of elements acting as identity outside Y. Tracing out a
non-identity Pauli factor gives zero, so ρ_Y = p^{−|Y|} Σ_{g∈G_Y} g|_Y. G_Y is
abelian and contains no nontrivial multiple of the identity, hence
Π_Y = |G_Y|^{−1} Σ_{g∈G_Y} g|_Y is a projector of rank p^{|Y|}/|G_Y| and
ρ_Y = (|G_Y|/p^{|Y|}) Π_Y is maximally mixed on its support;
S(Y) = |Y| − log_p |G_Y| (entropies in units of log p). For a party P put k = |P| − S(P) =
log_p |G_P|. The group G_P has k independent commuting generators; a Clifford
unitary U_P acting on the qudits of P maps them to Z_1, …, Z_k (any isotropic
subspace of the symplectic space of P can be mapped to the span of the first k
Z-coordinates; signs/phases are fixed by a Pauli correction). After U_P the
state is stabilized by Z_1, …, Z_k, i.e. it equals |0⟩^{⊗k} ⊗ |ψ′⟩ with |ψ′⟩ a
stabilizer state on the remaining qudits. U_P changes no entropy of a union of
parties (each such union contains all of P or none of it), and removing qudits
that are in a pure product state changes no entropy either. Repeat for every
party. ∎

**Lemma 2 (linearisation) [proved].** In normal form, h(X) := S(X) + Σ_{P∈X} S(P)
(X ⊆ {A,…,F}) is the rank function of a family of six subspaces of a vector
space over F_p.

*Proof.* Let L ⊂ F_p^{2N} be the Lagrangian subspace of the stabilizer state
(dim L = N); G_Y corresponds to L ∩ V_Y, where V_Y is the set of vectors
supported on the 2|Y| coordinates of Y, so S(Y) = |Y| − dim(L ∩ V_Y) (Lemma 1).
Let π_X be the projection onto the coordinates of the qudits in X. Then
dim π_X(L) = N − dim(L ∩ ker π_X) = N − dim(L ∩ V_{X^c}) = N − |X^c| + S(X^c) =
|X| + S(X). With U_P ⊆ L* the span of the coordinate functionals of party P
restricted to L, dim π_X(L) = dim(Σ_{P∈X} U_P). In normal form |X| =
Σ_{P∈X} S(P). ∎

(The same observation, phrased with phase-space distributions, is Gross–Walter
Thm 2.)

**Consequence [proved].** Every linear inequality f(h) ≥ 0 that holds for all
F_p-linear polymatroids on six elements yields an inequality
f′(S) := f(h_norm(S)) ≥ 0 valid on Stab₅, where h_norm(S)(X) = S(X) +
Σ_{P∈X} S(P). In particular every six-variable linear rank inequality — with the
purifier as one of the six variables — is valid on Stab₅.

**Common information [standard].** For subspaces, Z = U_X ∩ U_Y satisfies
h(XZ) = h(X), h(YZ) = h(Y), h(Z) = h(X) + h(Y) − h(X∪Y), and the seven-element
family (U_A, …, U_F, Z) is again linear, so its rank function satisfies all
Shannon inequalities. This is the method of DFZ and of
Hammer–Romashchenko–Shen–Vereshchagin.

## 3. The counterexample

**Theorem [certified].** Stab₅ ⊊ QLR₅. A witness is the ray

    r = (2,2,3,1,3,3,4,1,3,3,4,2,4,3,3,2,3,3,3,3,4,3,3,3,3,4,3,3,3,3,2)

(mask order; frontier index 275 in `data/a2_frontier300.npy`), which

* satisfies all 10,860 facet instances of QLR₅, with exact tight rank 30, so it
  is an extreme ray of QLR₅ (`data/a2_frontier300.npy`, gate G20);
* violates

      S(BCD) + S(ABCD) + S(ABE) + 2S(BCE) + 2S(ADE) + S(CDE)
        ≥ 2S(BC) + S(ABD) + S(ACD) + S(AE) + S(DE) + S(BCDE) + S(ABCDE)      (★)

  (left minus right equals −1 on r),

while (★) holds on Stab₅. Proof of (★): with Z the common information of AD and
BE, the nonnegative combination of Shannon inequalities on {A,…,F,Z}

    5·H(Z|ABCDEF) + 2·I(F;Z|ABCDE) + I(A;B|Z) + I(A;C|BFZ) + I(A;E|BCDZ)
    + I(A;Z|BCDEF) + I(A;Z|BE) + I(B;D|AEZ) + I(B;D|AZ) + I(B;Z|ACDEF)
    + I(B;Z|AF) + I(C;Z|BDEF) + I(C;Z|ADEF) + I(C;Z|ABDE) + I(C;Z|BE)
    + I(C;Z|ABD) + I(D;E|AFZ) + I(D;E|BCZ) + I(D;Z|ABCEF) + I(D;Z|BEF)
    + I(E;F|BZ) + I(E;Z|ABCF) + I(E;Z|AD) + I(F;Z|AE) + I(F;Z|AD) + I(F;Z|BC)

becomes, after substituting h(ADZ) = h(AD), h(BEZ) = h(BE), h(Z) = I(AD;BE),
a linear inequality in h alone, which equals (★) after h = h_norm(S) and
purification. The identity is checked in exact arithmetic
(`data/s17_exclusions.json`, `scripts/s17_certify.py`, `epr1kit/stabcert.py`).
Moreover (★) is a facet of the qubit stabilizer cone: 30 linearly independent
known qubit stabilizer entropy vectors attain equality
(`data/s17_ineq_tight_rank.npy`, class 29). ∎

## 4. Scale

* Frontier (all catalogue orbits with maximum coordinate ≤ 5 that are neither
  seeds nor pool-witnessed, plus q₂; 300 rays): 144 realised by explicit graph
  states (λ = 1/2/3: 126/17/1), 139 excluded by exact certificates as above,
  17 undecided (`data/a2_frontier_status.json`).
* **Whole A1′ catalogue (1,917,706 extreme-ray orbits of QLR₅), round R28:**
  a cutting-plane loop (single common information over disjoint pairs; every
  infeasibility turned into an exact certificate; new classes re-screened over
  the catalogue) classifies every orbit:
  1,914,541 (99.84 %) excluded from Stab₅, 599 realised, 2,566 undecided
  (`results/2026-10-01_s18-cutting-plane/`). The exclusions use 747 inequality
  classes up to S₆ (`data/s17_ineq_classes.npy`); 4,665 certificates beyond the
  139 frontier ones are stored in `data/s18_exclusions.npz` (integer multipliers).
* **Extreme rays of Stab₅.** An extreme ray of QLR₅ that lies in Stab₅ ⊆ QLR₅
  is an extreme ray of Stab₅. Hence the 599 realised orbits are extreme-ray
  orbits of the five-party stabilizer cone (59 were known; BCHS)
  — `data/stab5_extreme_reps.npy`.
* **Facets of Stab₅.** 119 of the 747 classes are certified facets of the qubit
  stabilizer cone: on each hyperplane lie 30 linearly independent known
  realisable vectors (`scripts/s19_facet_check.py`).
* Literature anchor: the six-variable inequalities (44)–(48) printed in DFZ §6
  (Theorem 3 type), applied with the purifier as the sixth variable, already
  exclude 45,292 catalogue orbits; of the frontier rays only (45) excludes one.
* Validation: no class is violated by any of 1,130,413 random 12-qubit graph
  states (two qubits per party), the 760 six-qubit graph states, 1,130 qutrit
  graph states, 200,000 random graph states with arbitrary party sizes (first
  60 classes), or any of the 747 realisation certificates.

## 5. Realisations and q₂

`scripts/s16_stab_search.py` searches graph states in normal form (Lemma 1;
every stabilizer state is LC-equivalent to a graph state [cited: Van den Nest,
Dehaene, De Moor, quant-ph/0308151, Thm 1]; graph-state entropies are GF(2)
cut ranks [cited: Hein, Eisert, Briegel, quant-ph/0307130, Prop. 3]). For fixed
λ this search space is complete. q₂ (the second edge of the face of SHC Table 3
ray 18) is **not** realisable at λ = 1 (with one qubit per party the realisable
vectors are exactly the 760 six-vertex graph-state vectors) and **is**
realisable at λ = 2 (a 12-qubit graph state; verified also by state-vector SVD).

## 6. Open questions

* **S7″**: is Stab₅ equal to QLR₅ cut by all single-common-information
  six-variable linear rank inequalities? **S7′**: by all six-variable linear rank
  inequalities? (The six-variable list is not known to be complete; DFZ report
  3,490 classes, some needing two common informations.) On the catalogue, every
  orbit that is single-CI feasible is either realised or still undecided — no
  orbit is known to be single-CI feasible and outside Stab₅. Longer annealing
  keeps converting undecided orbits into realised ones (R28: 89 of 261 with
  N ≤ 24 at 10 s instead of 3 s).
* The single-CI cone has extreme rays that are not extreme rays of QLR₅ (created
  by the cuts); they are not in the catalogue and are untested.
* Facets: 628 of the 747 classes are not yet certified as facets.
